# Abstract

## A Study on Portfolio Optimization Based on a Multi-Criteria Investment Priority Index for Power Equipment Investment Planning

Power asset investment planning is not merely a matter of replacing aged facilities. It is a multi-year portfolio decision problem in which risk, economic benefit, supply reliability, safety, environmental impact, annual budget, and replacement capacity must be considered simultaneously. Conventional risk-based prioritization is useful for identifying high-risk assets, but it does not sufficiently explain the cost-effectiveness of replacement, the long-term benefit after replacement, operating key performance indicators, or resource allocation among heterogeneous asset types.

This dissertation proposes a portfolio optimization method based on a multi-criteria investment priority index for power equipment investment planning. The proposed method starts from asset-level data in which probability of failure (PoF), consequence of failure (CoF), and risk have already been calculated. It then derives risk reduction, investment value, investment efficiency, SAIDI reduction, expected failure reduction, and expected safety and environmental impact. In this study, investment value is defined as the net benefit obtained by subtracting the present value of replacement cost from the risk reduction benefit over the design-life period after replacement. This definition makes it possible to distinguish assets with high current risk from assets that produce high investment benefit under limited resources.

The study also develops a Priority Index (PI) that integrates economic, reliability, safety, and environmental criteria. The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is used to estimate relative weights from pairwise comparison surveys of twenty experts, while fuzzy adjustment is applied to quantify the linguistic ambiguity inherent in expert judgment. This structure is not a fully automated decision model based only on numerical indicators. Rather, it is a human-in-the-loop quantitative decision structure that explicitly incorporates expert judgment into a mathematical optimization model.

A case simulation was conducted for a five-year planning horizon from 2026 to 2030. The analysis covers six major distribution asset types: pole-mounted transformers, ground-mounted transformers, overhead switchgear, underground switchgear, overhead distribution lines, and underground cables. Due to limitations in access to actual utility operation data, a synthetic input dataset was constructed by referring to the Common Network Asset Indices Methodology (CNAIM). Each investment alternative is defined as an asset-year combination, and common constraints are imposed on annual budget, annual replacement quantity, and at most one replacement per asset during the planning period.

The optimization simulation was conducted in a stepwise manner. First, risk-based prioritization and investment-value-based optimization were compared to examine the economic effect of introducing investment value. Second, asset-level PI-based optimization was performed to evaluate how the portfolio changes when reliability, safety, and environmental criteria are incorporated in addition to economic criteria. Third, an integrated PI-based system-level optimization was conducted by assigning asset-type weights to the common candidate pool and optimizing the entire set of heterogeneous assets as one portfolio. A candidate-limited ILP was also used as a comparative scenario that respects the first-stage asset-level PI selection results.

The simulation results show that risk-based prioritization is effective for identifying high-risk assets but has limitations in constructing a cost-effective investment portfolio. The investment-value ILP improved investment value by 16.2%, risk reduction by 8.8%, and SAIDI reduction by 10.5% compared with the risk-greedy baseline under nearly the same budget level. The PI-based ILP reduced investment value by 4.0% compared with the investment-value ILP, but improved SAIDI reduction by 13.0% and asset-level PI by 9.0%. The integrated ILP selected 5,473 assets with a total investment cost of 67.387 billion KRW, achieving an investment value of 112.434 billion KRW, an integrated PI of 935.03, and an investment efficiency of 2.669. Compared with the PI ILP, the integrated ILP improved investment value by 14.3%, risk reduction by 3.0%, asset-level PI by 27.9%, integrated PI by 44.6%, and investment efficiency by 16.4%.

Sensitivity and robustness analyses were performed for changes in budget, replacement capacity, SAIDI constraints, total risk constraints, combined KPI constraints, and operating-goal weights. Additional analyses were also conducted for the integrated PI mixing parameter α and for policy-driven minimum allocation constraints by asset type. A total of 246 sensitivity scenarios were implemented by applying 82 scenarios to three simulation scopes. The investment-value ILP outperformed the risk-greedy method in 67 out of 82 representative scenarios. The PI ILP outperformed the investment-value ILP in asset-level PI or SAIDI in all 82 representative scenarios, and the integrated ILP outperformed the PI ILP in integrated PI in all 82 representative scenarios. The integrated ILP also satisfied budget, quantity, SAIDI, and risk constraints in all 82 scenarios within the integrated PI portfolio scope. The additional policy-constraint analysis shows that minimum investment or replacement requirements can reflect practical allocation needs, while also creating an opportunity cost when the constraint level becomes excessive.

The academic contribution of this dissertation lies in structuring the post-risk stage of asset investment planning into investment value assessment, multi-criteria PI calculation, integrated PI formulation, and portfolio optimization. By combining AHP-based expert weighting, fuzzy adjustment, and integer linear programming, the study provides a decision process that links quantitative performance indicators with expert judgment. From a practical perspective, the proposed framework allows utilities to compare trade-offs among economic value, reliability, safety, environmental impact, and budget constraints when establishing a multi-year investment plan.

This study is limited in that the case analysis is based on a synthetic dataset constructed with reference to a public asset management methodology rather than actual utility operation data. Therefore, the numerical results should be interpreted as evidence of the procedural validity and decision-support potential of the proposed method, not as a direct replacement plan for a specific utility. Future research should validate the method using actual asset condition data, failure histories, outage restoration times, connected customers, replacement costs, regional construction conditions, and utility-specific operating KPIs.

**Keywords**: Power Equipment, Investment Planning, Investment Value, Investment Priority Index, Integrated PI, AHP, Fuzzy Adjustment, Portfolio Optimization, Integer Linear Programming, Human-in-the-loop
